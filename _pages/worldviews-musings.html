These are some short ideas I've articulated. I'm no expert philosopher, so there are gaps in some arguments.

Premises: Why we take things for granted

Because of how we experience life, I'd say every generation inherits a "lifestyle premise", a baseline of what counts as normal.

Progress feels like progress only to the generation that witnesses a baseline shift. Later generations receive that shift as a prior and treat it as ordinary.

Humans adapt to both abundance and deprivation, though adaptation can break under extreme conditions.

This helps explain why empathy does not scale cleanly: many people lack the lived reference points needed to reconstruct others' prior baselines.

Privilege as Network Topology

Privilege is often framed as possession (wealth, status), but through network theory it can be framed as position.

In this framing, proximity to hubs and shortcuts reduces distance to opportunities, information, and decision-makers.

Weak ties act as bridges between clusters, so small changes in network position can create disproportionately large changes in outcomes.

Two equally capable people can experience very different results because of where they sit in the network.
